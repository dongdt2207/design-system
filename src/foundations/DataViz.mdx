# Data colors

Three different jobs, three different palettes. Choose by **the job the color is doing**, not by taste.

| Palette | What it does | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Categorical | identity — separating discrete series | bars, lines, slices |
| Sequential | continuous magnitude | heatmaps, choropleths |
| Diverging | two opposing poles around a baseline | variance, growth above and below 0 |

Status colors (`--color-text-success`, `--color-text-danger`, …) **are not series colors**. They carry their own meaning, and they always travel with an icon and a label rather than standing on color alone.

## Categorical — 8 steps

Assign in **fixed order**, never cycling: the first series always takes `--viz-cat-1`. When a series is filtered out, the rest **keep their colors** — color belongs to the entity, not to the rank. If you need a ninth series, group into "Other" or split into small multiples; don't invent another hue.

Step 1 is the brand color `#287CF0`. Switch to dark to see the dark set — **chosen for dark backgrounds**, not flipped from the light one.

## Sequential — one hue, light → dark

<SemanticColors names={['--viz-seq-100', '--viz-seq-200', '--viz-seq-300', '--viz-seq-400', '--viz-seq-500', '--viz-seq-600', '--viz-seq-700']} />

Use the **whole range** for continuous magnitude, where the lightest step means "close to zero" and is allowed to sink into the background. For **ordinal steps** (funnels, rankings, ordered groups), the end nearest the background must separate from it: on light backgrounds start at `--viz-seq-400`, on dark backgrounds stop at `--viz-seq-600`.

## Diverging — two poles, gray in the middle

<SemanticColors names={['--viz-diverge-cool', '--viz-diverge-mid', '--viz-diverge-warm']} />

Blue ↔ red, two poles that read as opposites. The midpoint must be a **neutral gray** — it means "nothing here", so it cannot be a hue. Both wings get the same number of steps.

## Rules for drawing

- **One axis.** Never two y-scales on one chart. Two quantities on different scales become two charts, or get normalized to a common index.
- **Two or more series always get a legend**, and four or fewer get direct labels — identity never rests on color alone. With a single series the title already names it, so no legend is needed.
- **Text stays text.** Figures, labels, and legends use `--color-text-primary/secondary/muted`, never the series color. The small swatch beside them is what carries identity.
- Keep marks thin, let gridlines and axes recede, and don't print a number on every point.

## Four colors that need a label on light backgrounds

`--viz-cat-2` (2.98:1), `--viz-cat-3` (2.62:1), `--viz-cat-4` (2.02:1), and `--viz-cat-5` (2.51:1) fall below 3:1 against the cream background. They remain valid, but they **require a visible label or an accompanying data table** — readers must never depend on color alone. On dark backgrounds all 8 steps reach ≥ 3:1.
